Preface

Have you ever had your flaws targeted in the endgame and lost stones, or felt frustrated because your opponent clearly seemed to have a weakness but you had no idea how to play?

When the stones get crowded, moves and objectives you would normally see become invisible, and you miss chances or get knocked off your feet more often. It is a sad thing to play more than 100 moves from the opening and then have the game turned around by a moment of carelessness in the endgame. And it is just as disheartening to have a chance and be unable to go after it. That said, putting in reinforcing move after reinforcing move when there is no need to defend is also a losing way to play, and recklessly going after your opponent’s weaknesses when nothing is there is no better a policy.

This book is devoted specifically to the study of those "endgame weaknesses."

There are two ways to get stronger at endgame weaknesses.

① Accustoming your eyes and mind to positions with many stones tangled together

② Finding weaknesses from the shape

In this book, the problem diagrams have been made from positions close to those in the games you play as amateurs. By solving many

3

problems, your eyes and mind will gradually grow accustomed to them, until you can hunt for weaknesses even in crowded positions. In the introduction we also set out the key points about which shapes tend to become weaknesses, so as you solve the problems you will learn to judge from the shape as well.

The problems are set with the theme of going after weaknesses, but if defense is your weak suit, we also recommend solving them from the defender’s point of view, asking where you ought to defend.

The problems in Chapters 1 and 2 tell you how many weaknesses there are, so we think they will be relatively easy to find. If this area is your weak suit, first keep the shapes of weaknesses in mind and find the general objective. Then gradually work on practicing accurate reading.

Chapters 3 and 4 do not state the number of weaknesses, so the difficulty rises a little, but you can think about them with a feeling close to that of a real game. If you tackle the hard problems again and again, shapes you tend to overlook will start to become visible.

We hope that by working through this book, all of you will become strong at endgame weaknesses.

January 2016 — Yoshihara Yukari and O Yuinin
