Preface

Ever since my days as an insei I have enjoyed composing tsumego, and I have built up quite a collection of problems. That said, I mostly composed them to pose to my fellow insei and to professional players, for the pleasure of watching them squirm, so many of them may strike you as difficult.

This book is a tsumego collection for amateur players, 200 problems carefully chosen to be as easy to solve as possible. Every problem is “Black to play, White dies,” and a ko counts as a failure, so I think you can work through them with confidence.

Be warned, though: many of them look easy but conceal traps two and three layers deep (laughs). If you cannot solve one, feel free to look at the answer right away. By going through them repeatedly, the techniques should come to lodge themselves in your head naturally.

The problems are divided into ranks C through A, but since I was not thinking about difficulty when I composed them, please understand that these rankings are not strict.

If you can make a habit of quickly solving one problem from this book whenever you have a spare moment, your playing strength should improve enormously.

January 2015

Yamada Kimio

Yamada Kimio

Born in 1972. From Osaka Prefecture.

Student of Yamashita Jungen 7-dan. Became a professional in 1989, reached 9-dan in 2006. Affiliated with the Kansai Headquarters of the Nihon Ki-in.

1993: Shin’ei Tournament champion. 1997: 22nd Shin’jinō champion. Same year, won his first of the seven major titles at the 45th Ōza. 1998: Shin’ei Tournament champion. 1999: 8th Ryūsei champion. 2000: runner-up in the 5th Samsung Fire & Marine Insurance Cup. 2004: challenger for the 29th Gōsei. 2006: challenger for the 61st Honinbō. 2009 and 2010: challenger for the 57th and 58th Ōza. 2011: 58th NHK Cup champion.

Entered the Meijin League 7 times. Entered the Honinbō League 10 times.

1992: Kidō Award for Best Newcomer. 1994: Kidō Award for Most Games Played. 1996: Kidō Award for Best Win Rate.

1997: Kidō Award for Outstanding Player and Consecutive Win Record (18 consecutive wins). 2003: Kidō Award for Consecutive Win Record (18 consecutive wins). 2011: achieved 800 career wins.

Yamada Shihō 7-dan and Yamada Kazukio 7-dan are his older brothers.

His books include Yamada-Style Go Muscle Training and Sabaki Techniques to Gain 10 Points (Mynavi).
