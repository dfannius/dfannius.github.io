Preface

Hello, we are the Modern Go Research Institute. This time we have written a collection of practical problems devoted to sabaki, a technique that dramatically changes the quality of your fighting.

Sabaki means neutralizing your opponent’s attack and pursuit. Concretely, it is a technique in which "simply changing the direction of an atari produces a difference of ten points or more." Once you can put it to use in your own games, your strength is certain to improve dramatically.

As wonderful as this technique is, it seems that few players - even dan-level players - can play sabaki correctly in a real game. The main reason is that "in many situations, sacrificing stones is the premise." This runs directly counter to the basic principle of defending your stones so that they are not captured, so it is perfectly natural to feel some resistance to it.

That is why this book is organized so that you can study sabaki from the basics through advanced applications, divided into the two categories of shape and sacrifice stones. Grasp the rhythm of sabaki and pull ahead of your rivals.

The Importance of the Atari Kikashi

Chapter 1 focuses on how you should maneuver when your opponent starts something - on handling your stones in that kind of fighting situation.

Among the many points of technique, the one that feels most important is that the atari kikashi should be used with care. There are two things in particular you will want to keep in mind:

  • The direction in which you give atari
  • Keeping the option of not settling the atari

Simply being aware of these two things should broaden your chances of achieving sabaki, because almost every sabaki technique makes use of the atari kikashi. It is our hope that as you work through this chapter you will come to appreciate the merits of leaving the atari kikashi unplayed.

Afterword

Thank you for reading Go: 100 Sabaki Tesuji all the way to the end. This book is a problem collection packed with knowledge about sabaki, and we would be glad if it has added even one usable tactic to your repertoire.

To anyone who feels "my stock of knowledge has grown, but it is hard to put it to use in real games," the one piece of advice we would offer is: always keep the option of sacrificing stones in mind. When knowledge and imagination are combined, the frequency with which you can put them to use in real games is sure to rise.

Conversely, the way of thinking that most easily invites hardship is trying to rescue every last stone. In enemy territory especially, the difference in the number of stones means that taking on the fight head-on and coming away with a result that is even or better is difficult.

Sabaki tesuji hold your own overplays in check and at the same time offer hints for winning a fight comfortably, so please make active use of them in your games.
